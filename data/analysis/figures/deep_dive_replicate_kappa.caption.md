**Deep-dive records are reproducible across independent runs, and most run-to-run differences arise from which papers are retrieved.** Fifty proteins drawn at random from the deep-dive cohort were re-analyzed in two ways, and each of the 24 LLM-derived filter fields, together with the derived surface call and confidence tier, was compared with the published record: a full re-run with identical prompts three months after the published run, re-executing literature discovery, abstract triage, paper selection, section builders and synthesis (maroon, solid); and a fixed-evidence replay that reused each published record's selected evidence and re-ran only the section builders and synthesizer (lavender, dotted). Points show Cohen's κ against the published record; bars are 95% percentile intervals from 5,000 paired bootstrap resamples of proteins. Ordered fields, including the five-tier confidence spectrum, use linearly weighted κ; nominal fields and yes/no flags use unweighted κ. State dependence and co-receptor dependency are scored on their ordered levels only, excluding unclear and unknown calls (n shown). Green shading marks the moderate (0.60–0.79), strong (0.80–0.90) and almost perfect (>0.90) agreement ranges of McHugh (2012). The surface call agreed with κ = 0.82 on the full re-run and κ = 0.95 on the replay. Induction trigger (κ = 0.60) is recorded as a single dominant trigger although several can apply to one protein, and shed form (κ = 0.56) is rare (4 of 50 proteins), which deflates κ despite 92% raw agreement.
