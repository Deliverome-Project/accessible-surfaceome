**Supplementary Figure 13. Functional assays and flow cytometry provide most of the direct surface evidence, while immunofluorescence and immunohistochemistry mostly place proteins at the membrane without showing they are reachable from outside.** For the 3,035 deep-dived proteins called canonical or likely, each experiment the agent extracted from the cited literature is classified by method and judged direct (an antibody or probe reached the protein from outside an intact cell: live or non-permeabilized staining of an extracellular epitope or tag, membrane-impermeable labeling, or a functional readout that requires surface access) or supporting (places the protein at the plasma membrane without showing its outside is reachable: membrane-rim staining, fluorescent-protein fusions, or membranous immunohistochemistry). a) Proteins with direct evidence from each method (solid) and proteins for which that method reached only the supporting level (light). b) Share of the 2,203 proteins with any direct evidence for which the method was the only direct method; immunohistochemistry had two direct proteins, too few for a share. Colors group methods into imaging and cytometry, surface labeling or capture, functional surface assays, and other methods (for example, cell-surface ELISA, CITE-seq antibody tags, immuno-electron microscopy on intact cells, and membrane-impermeable crosslinking). Functional surface assays include toxin, antibody, ADC, or CAR-T activity against live target-positive cells, radioligand binding on live cells, ectodomain shedding, and transport of extracellular substrates.
