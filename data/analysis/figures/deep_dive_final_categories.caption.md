**Two-panel summary of how the deep-dive agent sorts the surface cohort, and how cell-state modulation cuts across every tier.** (a) The deep-dived cohort placed on a five-tier confidence spectrum: `canonical` (the strict gold-standard surface tier), `likely` (the broader passes-likely surface set), then the below-likely genes split by the deep-dive's tentative call — `low` (low/moderate accessibility but weak evidence — maybe surface), `no` (leaned not-surface), and `uncertain` (ambiguous). `canonical` and `likely` are exactly the frontend catalog-preset predicates (`passesCanonical` / `passesLikely`); the low/no/uncertain split of the negatives is a figure-only refinement (the presets don't cover the negatives). (b) Cell-state modulation is a **facet, not a tier**: within each surface tier (canonical / likely / low), the 100%-stacked bar splits proteins into **state-gated** (surface only when induced off a low/absent baseline, `low_endogenous_expression` = true) vs **constitutive baseline** (surface at rest and merely *further* cell-state-modulated — the ICAM1 class, `low_endogenous_expression` = false). The split is monotonic with confidence — canonical 81% constitutive / 19% state-gated (432 genes), likely 49% / 51%, low 30% / 70% — showing that cell-state variation reaches well into the canonical tier rather than being confined to `likely`; consistent with the PR #130 principle that *when* a protein is surface (state-dependence) is carried as a facet and does not gate *whether* it is a surface protein. Truly cell-state-invariant surface proteins are vanishingly rare (37 across all three tiers). Computed over the full deep-dive cohort (5,130 genes); `canonical` uses the PR #130 gate (overall confidence, not the A1-only evidence grade), giving 2,243 canonical / 624 likely / 1,134 low / 1,074 no / 55 uncertain.
