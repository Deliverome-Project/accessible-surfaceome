**Supplementary Figure 7. The Sonnet dual triage agent recovers near-complete coverage of clinically-validated surface targets — including targets no individual database calls — across three orthogonal positive-control sets.** Each panel is one set of independently-curated cell-surface targets, and each bar is the fraction of that set a given source labels surface: the five gating databases (UniProt and CSPA on their SurfaceBench-optimized cutoffs; GO CC, SURFY, HPA on their native cutoffs) and the two-stage Sonnet 4.6 triage agent (verdict yes or contextual across the genome-wide NCBI passes plus the PubMed rescue). a) Antibody–drug-conjugate targets (n = 234): the union of TheraSAbDab ADCs, Open Targets 26.06 ADC mechanism-of-action targets, and ADCdb antigens, HGNC-resolved, after removing non-cytotoxic conjugates (tedromer/celmoleukin/cafraglutide), Open Targets family-expansion artifacts, and ADCdb's secreted/intracellular antigens. b) T-cell engager targets (n = 30): TheraSAbDab CD3-binding bispecifics. c) ViralZone human viral entry receptors (n = 62). Sonnet covers 233/234 (99.6%) of ADC targets, 30/30 (100%) of TCE targets, and 61/62 (98.4%) of viral entry receptors — exceeding the best single database (UniProt in all cases) in every panel. Eight ADC targets and one TCE target sit in zero of the five databases yet are recovered by Sonnet (ASPH, FMOD, GSDME, HSPA1A, KLK2, KLK3, LRG1, MMP9) — contextual-surface biology (stress-induced chaperones, pyroptotic gasdermin, shed/conditionally-surface proteases) that the databases' steady-state definitions miss.
