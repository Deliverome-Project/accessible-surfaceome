**Supplementary Figure 3. Across model tiers and amounts of supplied context, triage accuracy saturates near 98% well before cost does, so the cheapest configuration reaching that plateau is the economical production choice.** Each point is one model–context combination, as in Supplementary Figure 2. The horizontal axis is the projected cost of triaging all 19,324 protein-coding genes once (log scale); the vertical axis is mean soft-credit accuracy on SurfaceBench. Cost is built from average tokens sent and returned per call at Anthropic’s October 2026 list prices, with shared instructions charged once at the first-use rate and reused cheaply, and each web search billed at one cent. Moving from Sonnet 4.6 to Opus 4.8 costs roughly 1.8 times as much for 0.7 percentage points, and adding a search tool costs more than moving to Opus — the two web-search configurations are among the three most expensive points on the chart. Production uses Sonnet 4.6 with identifiers, which matched Opus 4.8 accuracy at the lowest cost among the models available when the pipeline was built; of the later Claude 5.5 models, Haiku 5.5 came within a point of it at about a fourteenth of the cost, and Sonnet 5.5 was the most accurate configuration tested. Refused calls are excluded from accuracy and the refusal rate is labeled (about 5% for Opus 5.5 at its default reasoning effort and 4% at low effort; no other model refused). Color indicates model generation. The two lavender points are the production triage configuration and the PubMed-abstract variant used for the stage-two PubMed pass.
