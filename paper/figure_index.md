## Figure 4 — `deep_dive_flow`

- **Gist:** [gist](https://gist.github.com/beccajcarlson/eb7ce1015ff21ec0329472e33a9ea713) · `swh:1:rev:487f1aca9e01e732622fc22d5e380a7b0fd29ac9`

**Figure 4. Database and triage calls select 5,332 genes for the deep dive, which runs in three sequential stages, producing one validated record.** (a) Every protein-coding gene is assessed by the five reference databases and, in parallel, by the two-stage triage agent. The union of the two inputs is trimmed of proteins represented by a single database that the triage agent also called high-confidence non-surface with localization to clear non-surface compartments (noted in the figure), leaving 5,332 genes for the deep dive. (b) The per-gene deep-dive pipeline runs in three sequential stages, producing one validated record. Stage 2, block builders (Sonnet 4.6): builders draft the record's surface-evidence, surface-biology, and risk sections. Stage 3, synthesis (Sonnet 4.6): the synthesizer reads every section draft plus the Stage 1 evidence set, deterministic features, and the triage verdict, then writes the executive summary, structured filters, and confidence rationale.

**Supplementary Figure 4. Re-deriving each source database's surface-inclusion rule against SurfaceBench changes which proteins two of the five sources admit, while three are already well-calibrated.** Each panel shows one source database; points are alternative inclusion rules of increasing permissiveness, plotted by the number of proteins each would admit (horizontal axis, log scale; further left is stricter) against classification accuracy on SurfaceBench, the 147-protein benchmark of manually labeled surface, contextually surface, and non-surface proteins (vertical axis). Human Protein Atlas and SURFY rules were already at their accuracy optimum and were left unchanged, while GO’s gain in accuracy wasn’t justified based on the number of additional proteins added.
