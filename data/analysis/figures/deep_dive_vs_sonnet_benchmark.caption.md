**Supplementary Figure 11. For SurfaceBench proteins subjected to deep-dive analysis, the evidence-based assessment matched the soft-credit accuracy of Sonnet+NCBI triage, while the confidence tiers reflected the difficulty of each case.** For 113 of the 147 SurfaceBench benchmark protein-coding genes deep-dived, each protein's deep-dive surface call and the Sonnet+NCBI triage call are scored against the curated ground-truth verdict under the soft-credit rule — a contextually-surface protein counts correct when called surface. a) Overall soft-credit accuracy. b) Accuracy split by ground-truth bucket (yes / contextual / no). The deep-dive bar is stacked by the tier it assigned to the correctly-classified protein-coding genes — canonical / likely / low (green shades for canonical and likely, amber for low) and uncertain / no (grey), the same five-tier palette as Figure 5a — so its confidence composition is visible: ground-truth-yes genes are called mostly canonical and likely, contextual genes mostly likely, and the correct not-surface calls land in no. Sonnet+NCBI accuracy is the mean of three benchmark replicates (one dot per replicate; the error bar is the SEM across replicates); the deep dive runs once per protein, so its bar carries no replicate spread. The deep-dive verdict counts as surface when its tier is canonical, likely, or low; Sonnet and the ground truth count as surface when yes or contextual. The McNemar tests reported above find no significant difference in any bucket at this n.
